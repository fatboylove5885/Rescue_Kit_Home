Level 1: Microwave sweet potato with black beans

  • 5 min of effort
  • About 10 min total
  • Serves 2 to 3
  • Dishes: 1 plate

You'll need

  • 2 large sweet potatoes
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup shredded cheese
  • 1/2 cup salsa
  • Optional: sour cream or avocado

Here's how

  1. Scrub the potatoes under water and poke each one all over with a fork. Those holes let steam escape so nothing explodes.
  2. Put them on a microwave-safe plate. Microwave 6 to 8 minutes, flipping halfway, until a knife slides in with no fight.
  3. While they rest, warm the beans in a bowl for 1 minute.
  4. Split each potato down the middle. Pile on beans, cheese and salsa, and let the heat melt the cheese.

Good to know: Potatoes keep cooking for a minute after the microwave stops, so let them sit before you slice. No fresh ones? Microwave frozen cubed sweet potato in a bowl instead.

Level 2: Sheet-pan sausage and broccoli

  • 15 min of effort
  • About 40 min total
  • Serves 4
  • Dishes: 0 (foil-lined pan)

You'll need

  • 1 package (12 oz) fully cooked sausage, sliced into coins
  • 4 cups broccoli florets, fresh or frozen
  • 1 lb baby potatoes, halved
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp garlic powder, plus salt and pepper

Here's how

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F and line a sheet pan with foil. Foil is the whole "zero dishes" trick.
  2. Toss the potatoes with half the oil and a pinch of salt. Spread them cut side down and roast 10 minutes. They need a head start because they're the slowpokes.
  3. Add the broccoli and sausage, drizzle with the rest of the oil, sprinkle on the garlic powder and pepper, and toss right on the pan.
  4. Roast 15 to 20 more minutes, until the potatoes are fork-tender and the edges look browned.

Good to know: Cut the potatoes small and spread everything in a single layer. Crowded pans steam instead of roast, and steamed broccoli is a sad broccoli. More pan meals in our easy sheet-pan dinners guide.

Level 3: Slow cooker salsa chicken

  • 5 min of effort
  • 3 to 7 hours, hands off
  • Serves 6
  • Dishes: 1 (0 with a liner)

You'll need

  • 2 lb boneless, skinless chicken breasts
  • 1 jar (16 oz) salsa
  • 1 packet taco seasoning
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained
  • For serving: tortillas, rice, shredded cheese

Here's how

  1. Spray the slow cooker or add a liner. Lay the chicken in the bottom (thawed, please).
  2. Sprinkle the taco seasoning over the top, then pour on the salsa and beans.
  3. Cover and cook on LOW 6 to 7 hours, or HIGH 3 to 4 hours, until the chicken reaches 165°F in the thickest part. Keep the lid closed. Every peek adds about 20 minutes.
  4. Shred it with two forks right in the pot and stir it back into the sauce.
  5. Serve in tortillas, over rice, on a baked potato, or straight from a spoon at the counter. We won't tell.

Good to know: Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge and freeze well for up to 3 months. Reheat to 165°F. For safe temperatures on other meats, see the USDA safe minimum cooking temperature chart. More hands-off ideas: slow cooker dinners for busy families.

Cheap dinners that don't taste cheap

Budget cooking isn't about eating sadly. It's about knowing where your money goes. Here are two dinners built from pantry staples, plus the little habits that make groceries last. For a deeper dive, read our guide to cheap family dinners with the cost per serving, or see the USDA MyPlate healthy-eating-on-a-budget tips.

Egg fried rice (better than takeout and done in 15)

  • 15 min
  • Serves 4
  • Dishes: 1 skillet

You'll need

  • 3 cups cooked rice, ideally cold, or 2 microwave rice pouches
  • 4 eggs, beaten
  • 1 cup frozen peas and carrots
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Optional: sliced scallions

Here's how

  1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the frozen vegetables and cook 2 to 3 minutes, stirring.
  2. Push the vegetables to one side. Pour the eggs into the empty space and scramble until fully set, with no runny bits.
  3. Add the rice and break up any clumps with your spoon. Cook 3 to 4 minutes, until hot and just starting to crisp.
  4. Splash in the soy sauce, toss everything together and top with scallions.

Good to know: Cold rice fries better than fresh, because fresh rice turns gummy. Leftover rice from last night is the secret ingredient.

Pantry tomato pasta with white beans and spinach

  • 20 min
  • Serves 4
  • Dishes: 1 pot and a colander

You'll need

  • 12 oz pasta
  • 1 jar (24 oz) marinara sauce
  • 1 can (15 oz) white beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 cups baby spinach, or 1 cup thawed frozen spinach
  • Parmesan for the top

Here's how

  1. Boil the pasta in salted water according to the package. Scoop out 1/2 cup of the pasta water before you drain.
  2. Return the empty pot to the stove over medium-low heat. Add the marinara, beans, spinach and a splash of pasta water.
  3. Simmer 3 to 4 minutes, until the spinach wilts and the beans are hot.
  4. Stir in the pasta, toss to coat and top with parmesan.

Good to know: Beans add protein and make one jar of sauce feed more people. Frozen spinach is cheaper than fresh and works just as well here.

Three stretch tricks

  • Buy the big bag, then portion it. A large bag of rice or pasta costs less per serving than small boxes.
  • Add beans, lentils or eggs. They stretch meat dishes and make meatless nights feel like real dinner.
  • Freeze the second half. Soups, chili and casseroles freeze beautifully for up to 3 months. See our freezer meals for busy weeknights for what freezes best.

The pantry hero shopping list

If you stock only a handful of things, make it these. They carry most cheap dinners, and our pantry staples list for budget cooking goes deeper.

  • Rice and pasta for the base of almost everything.
  • Canned beans for cheap protein and fiber.
  • Eggs for breakfast-for-dinner and fried rice.
  • Frozen vegetables that never wilt in the crisper.
  • Canned tomatoes or jarred marinara for sauces and soups.
  • Onions and garlic for flavor on a dime.

Seven tired-night tips that actually help

  1. Decide dinner before you're hungry. Hungry you makes terrible, expensive choices. Calm you at 10 a.m. is a genius.
  2. Match the recipe to your energy, not your ambition. Fancy dinner on a Wednesday is how takeout wins.
  3. Buy the shortcut. Rotisserie chicken, rice pouches and frozen veggies aren't cheating. They're staff.
  4. Line the pan with foil. Future you will be so grateful that you'll thank past you out loud.
  5. Cook once, eat twice. Double the slow cooker chicken and tomorrow's tacos are already done.
  6. Keep a "never again" list. If a recipe made you cry or cost 14 ingredients, delete it from your life.
  7. Write the plan where everyone can see it. A fridge list ends "I don't know, what do you want?" forever.

Plan a whole week of dinners in 10 minutes

  1. Look at your calendar first. Soccer night and late-meeting night get the easiest dinners.
  2. Pick one slow cooker or dump-and-bake night for the busiest day.
  3. Choose two "no-brainer" nights, like quesadillas or breakfast for dinner.
  4. Make a list of what's missing. Check the pantry before you shop, since it's tempting to buy a fifth jar of cumin.
  5. Write the plan where everyone can see it. A fridge list ends the "what do you want?" loop.

The picky-eater peace treaty

  • Serve it deconstructed. Tacos, baked potatoes and pasta bars let everyone build their own plate.
  • Keep one safe food on the table. Bread, rice or fruit means no one leaves hungry.
  • Offer, don't push. Put new foods out with no pressure. It can take many tries before a kid accepts something.
  • Let them help. Kids eat more of what they stirred, sprinkled or named.

One chicken, three dinners

Leftovers don't have to be the same dinner again. Start with one rotisserie chicken or a batch of slow cooker chicken and remix it. More ideas: leftover chicken recipes.

  1. Night 1: Chicken plate. Serve with rice and steamed or microwaved vegetables.
  2. Night 2: Quesadillas or wraps. Fold shredded chicken, cheese and salsa into tortillas and crisp in a skillet 2 to 3 minutes per side.
  3. Night 3: Quick soup. Simmer 4 cups broth with frozen vegetables 10 minutes, add the last of the chicken and a handful of noodles or rice, and heat until hot throughout.

Good to know: Cool leftovers within 2 hours and store in airtight containers for 3 to 4 days. Reheat to 165°F. Full guidance from USDA FSIS on leftovers and food safety.

Bonus recipe: pesto pasta with tomatoes

  • 15 min
  • Serves 4
  • 1 pot and a colander

You'll need: 12 oz rotini or penne, 1 jar (6 oz) pesto, 1 cup cherry tomatoes (halved), 1/2 cup grated parmesan. Optional: 2 cups shredded rotisserie chicken.

  1. Boil the pasta in salted water per the package. Scoop out 1/2 cup of pasta water, then drain.
  2. Return the pasta to the pot off the heat. Stir in the pesto and a splash of pasta water until creamy.
  3. Fold in the tomatoes, chicken and parmesan and serve.

New Level 1: Cheesy black bean quesadillas

  • 5 min of effort
  • About 10 min total
  • Serves 2
  • Dishes: 1 skillet

You'll need

  • 4 flour tortillas
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded cheese
  • Salsa for dipping

Here's how

  1. Mash half the beans with a fork and stir in the rest whole.
  2. Spread beans and cheese over half of each tortilla and fold in half.
  3. Cook in a dry skillet over medium heat 2 to 3 minutes per side, until golden and the cheese melts.
  4. Slice into wedges and serve with salsa.

Good to know: Mashing some of the beans acts like glue and keeps the filling from sliding out.

New Level 2: Sheet-pan chicken fajitas

  • 15 min of effort
  • About 35 min total
  • Serves 4
  • Dishes: 0 (foil-lined pan)

You'll need

  • 1 1/2 lb boneless chicken breast, sliced into strips
  • 3 bell peppers, sliced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 packet fajita or taco seasoning
  • Tortillas and toppings for serving

Here's how

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F and line a sheet pan with foil.
  2. Toss the chicken, peppers and onion with the oil and seasoning right on the pan.
  3. Roast 20 to 25 minutes, stirring once, until the chicken reaches 165°F and the vegetables have browned edges.
  4. Serve in warm tortillas with your favorite toppings.

Good to know: Slice the chicken thin so it cooks at the same pace as the vegetables.

New Level 3: Slow cooker chicken noodle soup

  • 10 min of effort
  • 6 to 8 hours, hands off
  • Serves 6
  • Dishes: 1 (0 with a liner)

You'll need

  • 1 1/2 lb boneless chicken breasts or thighs
  • 6 cups chicken broth
  • 3 carrots and 3 celery stalks, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 tsp garlic powder, plus salt and pepper
  • 2 cups egg noodles

Here's how

  1. Add the chicken, vegetables, broth and seasonings to the slow cooker.
  2. Cover and cook on LOW 6 to 8 hours, or HIGH 3 to 4 hours, until the chicken reaches 165°F.
  3. Shred the chicken with two forks and return it to the pot.
  4. Stir in the noodles, cover and cook on HIGH 15 to 20 minutes, until tender.

Good to know: Add the noodles at the end so they don't turn to mush. Leftover soup freezes well without the noodles.

Can I freeze these recipes?
