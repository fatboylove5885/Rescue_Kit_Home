22 Cozy Fall Dinners That Taste Like Wearing Your Favorite Sweater

Easy fall dinner ideas with real, step-by-step recipes, sorted by the tool you feel like using tonight.

There's a moment in October when your kitchen stops smelling like summer and starts smelling like decisions. The windows stay shut. The oven stops being an appliance and becomes a personality trait. And somewhere around 5:15 p.m., you realize you want dinner to do what a good sweater does: wrap around you, run a little too warm, and make you say "I'm not leaving" before you've even sat down.

That's the whole idea here. Fall food isn't about being fancy. It's soft, savory, a little sweet and forgiving. A sweater doesn't care if you're wearing it wrong, and neither do these recipes. If you've never cooked anything more ambitious than toast, you're in the right place. Every recipe below tells you what to buy, what to do in what order, and what it should look, smell and sound like at each stage, so you're never guessing.

I sorted all 22 by cooking method: soups, casseroles, slow cooker and sheet pan. Dreading the chopping? Head to the slow cooker. Only want one pan to wash? Sheet pan section, right this way. Each recipe lists time and cost. Cost key (rough estimate for a family of four; prices vary by store): $ = under about $12, $$ = about $12 to $20.

Before you start: a five-line beginner cheat sheet

  • Simmer = small, lazy bubbles breaking the surface now and then. A full rolling boil is the soup equivalent of a toddler tantrum.
  • Sauté = cook in a bit of fat over medium heat, stirring so nothing burns. You'll hear a gentle sizzle. Loud crackling means turn the heat down.
  • Fork-tender = a fork slides into the food with no resistance, like a spoon into soft butter.
  • Rest = leave the food alone for a few minutes after cooking so juices settle and you don't burn your mouth. It's mandatory, not optional.
  • Thermometer? A cheap instant-read one is the best $12 a beginner cook can spend. Chicken and turkey are safe at 165°F, pork chops at 145°F plus a 3-minute rest, ground beef at 160°F.

Quick plan: pick one dinner from each section for the week. After the recipes you'll find three cheap tricks that make everything taste like you tried harder, plus a free Fall Dinner Bingo card.

Soups That Count as a Hug

Soup is the original sweater. It's warm, it covers you completely, and nobody has ever said "I regret making a pot of soup." Six dinners, all on the stovetop, all forgiving.

1. Creamy Tomato Soup with Grilled Cheese Croutons

SERVES 4 · 30 MIN · $ · STOVETOP

Plain tomato soup is a polite, well-mannered bowl. Tomato soup with grilled cheese croutons is that same bowl arriving with a marching band. The croutons are tiny life rafts, except no one wants to be rescued.

What you'll need

  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 can (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes
  • 2 cups chicken or vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/4 tsp pepper
  • 4 slices sandwich bread
  • 4 slices cheddar or American cheese
  • 2 tbsp soft butter (for the sandwiches)

How to make it

  1. Melt 2 tbsp butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 to 6 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and see-through (translucent), not browned.
  2. Stir in the garlic and cook just 30 seconds. You'll smell it right away. Garlic burns fast, so move on as soon as the kitchen smells amazing.
  3. Pour in the tomatoes with all their juice, the broth, sugar, salt and pepper. Use your spoon to squish the tomatoes against the side of the pot.
  4. Bring to a gentle simmer, then lower the heat and cook, partly covered, for 15 minutes.
  5. Blend until smooth. An immersion (stick) blender is easiest: dip it straight in the pot. With a regular blender, work in batches, fill it no more than halfway, and hold a folded towel over the lid. Hot soup builds steam pressure and can pop the lid off.
  6. Return the soup to the pot over low heat and stir in the cream. Taste and add salt if it needs it. Keep it warm while you make the croutons.
  7. Spread the soft butter on the outside of each bread slice. Put cheese between two slices, buttered sides out. Cook in a skillet over medium-low heat, 3 to 4 minutes per side, until deep golden and the cheese has melted.
  8. Slice the sandwiches into 1-inch squares. Ladle soup into bowls and pile on the croutons right before serving, so they stay crisp.

Sweater tip: Low and slow is the secret to grilled cheese. High heat burns the bread before the cheese melts, and nobody wants a charcoal sandwich.

2. Sausage, Kale and White Bean Soup

SERVES 4 to 6 · 35 MIN · $ · STOVETOP

Italian sausage is the friend who walks into the room and instantly makes everything more interesting. You barely need other seasonings. Brown it, add beans and greens, and the soup tastes like it simmered all afternoon while you were, in fact, scrolling your phone.

What you'll need

  • 1 lb Italian sausage (mild or spicy), casings removed
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp dried Italian seasoning
  • 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 6 cups chicken broth
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) white beans (cannellini), drained
  • 1 bunch kale (about 4 packed cups), stems removed, leaves chopped
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan, for serving
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Heat a large pot over medium-high heat. Add the sausage (squeeze it out of the casings if needed) and break it into small crumbles with a wooden spoon. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, until browned with no pink left.
  2. If there's a lot of grease pooled in the pot, spoon out all but about 1 tablespoon. A little fat equals flavor, a lot equals greasy soup.
  3. Add the onion to the same pot and cook 4 minutes, scraping up the brown bits stuck to the bottom. That's the flavor.
  4. Stir in the garlic, Italian seasoning and red pepper flakes for 30 seconds.
  5. Pour in the broth and add the beans. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer for 15 minutes.
  6. Stir in the kale and cook 5 minutes more, until it's tender and dark green. Taste, then add salt and pepper. Sausage and broth are salty, so taste before you season.
  7. Serve topped with Parmesan.

Sweater tip: For a thicker, creamier soup without any cream, scoop out about 1/2 cup of the beans, mash them against the side of the pot with a fork, and stir them back in.

3. Butternut Squash Soup with Sage Brown Butter

SERVES 4 · 45 MIN · $$ · STOVETOP + OVEN

This is the soup you make when your in-laws are coming over and you'd like some credit. It tastes like a restaurant starter, but it's really just roasted squash, a blender and one fancy-sounding butter. We will not be correcting anyone who calls you a chef.

What you'll need

  • 2 packages (20 oz each) pre-cut butternut squash cubes, or 1 whole squash (about 2.5 lb), peeled and cubed
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 1 yellow onion, chopped
  • 4 cups vegetable or chicken broth
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon or nutmeg
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper
  • For the brown butter: 4 tbsp butter
  • 8 to 10 fresh sage leaves

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the squash on a sheet pan with 1 tbsp olive oil, the salt and pepper.
  2. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, flipping once halfway, until a fork slides in easily and the edges are browned. Browned edges equal flavor, so don't pull it out early.
  3. While the squash roasts, warm the remaining 1 tbsp oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion 5 minutes until soft.
  4. Add the roasted squash, broth and cinnamon. Simmer for 10 minutes.
  5. Blend until completely smooth (see the blender safety note in recipe 1). If the soup is too thick, thin it with a splash of water or broth, a few tablespoons at a time. Taste and adjust the salt.
  6. Make the brown butter: melt the butter in a small light-colored pan over medium heat. It will foam, then the foam settles and you'll see golden-brown flecks and smell something nutty. That takes 3 to 5 minutes.
  7. Add the sage leaves and cook 30 seconds, until crisp. Remove from the heat right away.
  8. Ladle the soup into bowls and spoon the brown butter and sage on top.

Sweater tip: Don't walk away from the butter. It goes from golden to burnt in about 20 seconds. If it smells burnt rather than nutty, toss it and start again. It costs 50 cents to try twice.

4. Chicken and Dumpling Soup

SERVES 4 to 6 · 45 MIN · $$ · STOVETOP

Dumplings are basically biscuits that went swimming. They float on top, steam from underneath and puff up like little clouds. The only rule is the hardest: once the lid goes on, you do not peek. Dumplings are shy.

What you'll need

  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, sliced into thin coins
  • 2 celery stalks, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 6 cups chicken broth
  • 3 cups shredded rotisserie chicken
  • 1 tsp dried thyme, 1 bay leaf
  • Dumplings: 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, 2 tsp baking powder, 1/2 tsp salt, 3/4 cup milk, 3 tbsp melted butter

How to make it

  1. Melt the butter in a large, wide pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots and celery and cook 6 to 7 minutes until the onion is soft.
  2. Add the garlic and stir 30 seconds. Sprinkle in the 2 tbsp flour and stir for 1 minute. This thickens the soup slightly.
  3. Slowly pour in the broth while stirring so no lumps form. Add the chicken, thyme and bay leaf. Simmer 10 minutes.
  4. While it simmers, make the dumpling dough: whisk the flour, baking powder and salt in a bowl. Stir in the milk and melted butter until just combined. Lumps are fine. Overmixing makes tough dumplings.
  5. Make sure the soup is at a steady, gentle simmer. Drop heaping tablespoons of dough on top, spaced apart (they'll puff up).
  6. Cover with a tight lid and cook 12 to 15 minutes. Do not lift the lid. Steam is what cooks them.
  7. Check one by cutting it open: the center should look fluffy and dry, not gummy. Remove the bay leaf and serve right away.

Sweater tip: A rotisserie chicken is the best $8 shortcut in the grocery store. Save the bones in the freezer for broth later.

5. Loaded Baked Potato Soup

SERVES 4 · 40 MIN · $ · STOVETOP

A baked potato walked into a soup pot and decided to become the main character. This one has bacon, cheese, sour cream and chives, which is to say all the things you'd normally pile on a potato, minus the foil and the 60-minute wait.

What you'll need

  • 6 slices bacon, chopped
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 lb russet potatoes (about 4 medium), peeled, cut in 3/4-inch cubes
  • 4 cups chicken broth
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded cheddar, plus more for topping
  • Chopped chives or green onion
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Add the chopped bacon to a cold large pot, then turn the heat to medium. Cook 8 to 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until crisp. Scoop the bacon onto a paper towel-lined plate, leaving about 1 tablespoon of bacon fat in the pot.
  2. Add the onion to the bacon fat and cook 5 minutes, then stir in the garlic for 30 seconds.
  3. Add the potatoes and broth. Bring to a boil, then lower to a simmer and cook 15 to 18 minutes, until the potatoes are fork-tender.
  4. Use a potato masher to mash about half of the potatoes right in the pot. This makes it creamy but still chunky.
  5. Turn the heat to low. Stir in the milk and sour cream, then add the cheese a handful at a time, stirring until melted. Don't let it boil after adding the dairy, or it can turn grainy.
  6. Taste and season with salt and pepper. Serve topped with bacon, extra cheese and chives.

Sweater tip: Set out the toppings in little bowls and let everyone build their own. People will eat anything they assembled themselves.

6. Turkey Pumpkin Chili

SERVES 6 · 45 MIN · $$ · STOVETOP

A can of pumpkin sounds like a plot twist. It isn't. It makes chili silky and rich, adds a gentle sweetness and disappears into the pot like a good stagehand. No one will say "I taste pumpkin." They'll just say "Why is this so good?"

What you'll need

  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 1 bell pepper, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 1/4 lb ground turkey
  • 2 tbsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 1 can (15 oz) pure pumpkin puree (NOT pie filling)
  • 1 can kidney beans and 1 can black beans, drained
  • 2 cups chicken broth
  • 1 tsp salt

How to make it

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Add the ground turkey and break it up as it cooks. Cook 7 to 8 minutes, until no pink remains.
  2. Add the onion and bell pepper. Cook 5 minutes until soft.
  3. Stir in the garlic, chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika and cinnamon. Cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly. Toasting the spices wakes them up.
  4. Add the tomatoes, pumpkin, beans, broth and salt. Stir well.
  5. Bring to a boil, lower to a simmer, and cook uncovered for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick.
  6. Taste and add more salt or chili powder if you like. Serve with shredded cheese, sour cream, tortilla chips or sliced green onion.

Sweater tip: Check the label: you want "100% pure pumpkin," not "pumpkin pie filling." One of those has sugar and spices already added, and your chili will taste like dessert.

Casseroles and Bakes

The casserole is the oven's love language. You assemble it, slide it in, and then enjoy a 20-minute window of doing absolutely nothing. Six dinners that bubble, brown and make your house smell like someone's grandma lives there.

7. Pumpkin Baked Ziti

SERVES 6 · 55 MIN · $ · OVEN

This is regular baked ziti wearing its fall costume. The pumpkin turns the sauce a gorgeous orange-pink and makes it creamy without making it taste like pumpkin pie. It's the Halloween costume that's actually good.

What you'll need

  • 12 oz ziti or penne pasta
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 jar (24 oz) marinara sauce
  • 1 cup pure pumpkin puree
  • 1/2 cup milk or heavy cream
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup ricotta cheese (optional)
  • 2 cups shredded mozzarella
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F. Lightly grease a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  2. Boil the pasta in well-salted water for 2 minutes LESS than the package says (it will finish cooking in the oven). Drain.
  3. In the same pot, heat the oil over medium heat. Cook the onion 5 minutes, then add the garlic for 30 seconds.
  4. Stir in the marinara, pumpkin, milk, Italian seasoning and salt. Simmer 5 minutes, stirring.
  5. Add the pasta to the sauce and stir until every piece is coated.
  6. Pour half into the baking dish. Dot with spoonfuls of ricotta (if using) and half the mozzarella. Add the rest of the pasta, then top with the remaining mozzarella and the Parmesan.
  7. Cover with foil and bake 20 minutes. Remove the foil and bake 10 to 15 minutes more, until the cheese is bubbling and has golden-brown spots.
  8. Let it rest 5 minutes before scooping. It will hold together better and won't burn your tongue.

Sweater tip: Slightly undercooking the pasta is the number one trick for baked pasta. Fully cooked noodles turn mushy after 30 minutes in the oven.

8. Chicken, Broccoli and Rice Casserole

SERVES 6 · 50 MIN · $ · OVEN

This is the casserole of potlucks and Tuesday nights, the one that said "don't worry, I've got you" long before it was trendy. Homemade cheese sauce sounds scary, but it's just butter, flour and milk doing a very slow dance.

What you'll need

  • 3 cups cooked rice (about 1 cup uncooked)
  • 3 cups cooked, chopped chicken
  • 4 cups small broccoli florets
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 cups milk
  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded cheddar, divided
  • 1/2 tsp garlic powder
  • Salt and pepper
  • 1/2 cup panko breadcrumbs mixed with 1 tbsp melted butter

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F and grease a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  2. Microwave or steam the broccoli for 2 to 3 minutes, just until bright green. It will finish cooking in the oven.
  3. Make the sauce: melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and cook 1 minute, whisking constantly. It will look like wet sand.
  4. Slowly pour in the milk and broth, whisking the whole time to prevent lumps. Simmer 3 to 5 minutes until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  5. Remove from heat. Stir in 1 cup of the cheddar, the garlic powder, 1/2 tsp salt and some pepper until melted.
  6. Fold in the rice, chicken and broccoli. Pour into the baking dish.
  7. Sprinkle with the remaining 1/2 cup cheddar, then the buttered panko.
  8. Bake 25 minutes uncovered, until bubbly around the edges and golden on top.

Sweater tip: Use leftover rice or the microwave pouch kind. Hot, freshly cooked rice makes the casserole mushy.

9. Shepherd's Pie with a Mashed Potato Blanket

SERVES 6 · 1 HR 10 MIN · $$ · OVEN

Technically, a pie made with beef is a cottage pie, and a true shepherd's pie uses lamb. We're not going to tell the beef. It's a savory meat-and-veggie filling tucked under fluffy mashed potatoes, a literal blanket, so you're essentially tucking your dinner in.

What you'll need

  • 2 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut in 1 1/2-inch chunks
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced small
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup beef broth
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F. Put the potatoes in a pot, cover with cold water, add a big pinch of salt, and bring to a boil. Cook 15 to 20 minutes, until a fork slides in with no resistance.
  2. Drain the potatoes. Return them to the pot, add the butter, milk, 1 tsp salt and some pepper, and mash until smooth. Set aside.
  3. In a large skillet over medium-high heat, cook the beef 6 to 8 minutes, breaking it up, until no pink remains. Drain off excess grease.
  4. Add the onion and carrots and cook 5 minutes until softened. Stir in the garlic and tomato paste and cook 1 minute.
  5. Sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute. Pour in the broth, Worcestershire and thyme. Simmer 5 minutes until thick like gravy. Stir in the peas and season with salt and pepper.
  6. Pour the filling into a 2.5-quart baking dish (or a 9x9-inch dish). Spoon the potatoes on top and spread to the edges so they seal in the filling. Drag a fork across the top to make ridges.
  7. Set the dish on a baking sheet to catch drips. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the filling bubbles at the edges and the ridges are golden. Rest 5 minutes.

Sweater tip: The fork ridges aren't just for looks. The peaks get crispy and golden, and the crispy peaks are the best part. Fight your family for them.

10. Cheesy Chicken Enchilada Bake

SERVES 6 · 50 MIN · $ · OVEN

Regular enchiladas ask you to roll twelve tortillas and wonder why you ever started. This version is a lasagna that took a vacation south of the border. No rolling, no tearing, no regrets, just layers.

What you'll need

  • 3 cups shredded cooked chicken
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 cans (10 oz each) red enchilada sauce
  • 1 cup salsa
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 8 small (6-inch) corn or flour tortillas
  • 2 cups shredded Mexican-blend or cheddar cheese
  • Toppings: sour cream, cilantro, sliced avocado

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F.
  2. In a bowl, mix the chicken, beans, salsa, cumin and about 1/2 cup of the enchilada sauce.
  3. Spread 1/2 cup of enchilada sauce on the bottom of a 9x13-inch dish. This keeps the bottom tortillas from sticking.
  4. Layer 4 tortillas (tear them to fill gaps), then half the chicken mixture, a drizzle of sauce and a third of the cheese. Repeat with the remaining tortillas and chicken mixture.
  5. Pour the rest of the sauce over the top and sprinkle with the remaining cheese.
  6. Cover with foil and bake 20 minutes. Uncover and bake 10 minutes more, until the cheese is bubbly and lightly browned.
  7. Rest 5 minutes so it cuts into neat squares. Top with sour cream, cilantro and avocado.

Sweater tip: Cut into squares like lasagna. The first piece never looks pretty, but it tastes the same. That's the cook's snack.

11. French Onion Chicken and Rice Bake

SERVES 4 to 6 · 1 HR 30 MIN · $$ · STOVETOP + OVEN

French onion soup without the part where the cheese fuses your bowl to the table. It's one pot, so there's only one thing to wash, and the onions do all the talking. They'll take 20 minutes to caramelize, and that's the whole secret: you can't rush a good sweater, either.

What you'll need

  • 6 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (about 1 1/2 lb)
  • 3 tbsp olive oil or butter, divided
  • 3 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 cup long-grain white rice, rinsed
  • 2 cups hot beef or chicken broth
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded Gruyère or Swiss cheese
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F. Pat the chicken dry with paper towels (dry chicken browns, wet chicken steams) and season both sides with salt and pepper.
  2. In a large oven-safe pot or deep skillet with a lid, heat 1 tbsp oil over medium-high heat. Sear the chicken 3 minutes per side until golden. It doesn't need to be cooked through. Move it to a plate.
  3. Lower the heat to medium-low. Add the remaining oil and the onions with a big pinch of salt. Cook 20 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until deep golden brown and jammy. If they stick, add a splash of water.
  4. Stir in the thyme, garlic and Worcestershire for 1 minute. Add the rice and stir for 1 minute.
  5. Pour in the hot broth and scrape the bottom of the pot to loosen the browned bits. Nestle the chicken on top of the rice.
  6. Cover tightly and bake 35 to 40 minutes, until the rice is tender and the chicken reaches 165°F.
  7. Sprinkle the cheese over the top, return to the oven uncovered for 5 minutes until melted and bubbly. Rest 5 minutes.

Sweater tip: Use HOT broth. Cold broth slows everything down and the rice may cook unevenly. Microwave it for 2 minutes first.

12. Sausage and Apple Stuffing Bake

SERVES 6 · 1 HR 10 MIN · $ · OVEN

This is Thanksgiving's greatest hits on a random Tuesday, with none of the turkey-related stress. Sweet apple, savory sausage and sage walk into a baking dish and come out as the most comforting thing you'll eat all month.

What you'll need

  • 1 lb bulk pork sausage
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 2 firm apples (Honeycrisp or Granny Smith), diced
  • 1 tbsp fresh chopped sage or 1 tsp dried
  • 8 cups cubed bread (about 1 lb loaf), dried out
  • 1 1/2 cups chicken broth
  • 2 large eggs
  • Salt and pepper

How to make it

  1. Dry the bread: spread the cubes on a sheet pan and bake at 300°F for 15 minutes (or leave out overnight). Dry bread soaks up flavor instead of turning into paste.
  2. Raise the oven to 350°F and butter a 9x13-inch dish.
  3. Cook the sausage in a large skillet over medium-high heat 6 to 8 minutes, breaking it up, until browned with no pink. Move it to a large bowl with a slotted spoon.
  4. In the same skillet, melt the butter. Cook the onion, celery and apples 6 to 8 minutes until softened. Stir in the sage for 30 seconds. Add all of it to the bowl.
  5. Add the bread cubes to the bowl. In a small bowl, whisk the broth, eggs, 1/2 tsp salt and a few grinds of pepper, and pour over everything. Toss gently until evenly moist.
  6. Spread into the dish. Cover with foil and bake 25 minutes. Remove the foil and bake 15 to 20 minutes more, until the top is golden and crisp.

Sweater tip: Taste a bit of the sausage before adding the salt. Some brands are salty enough to season the whole pan.

Slow-Cooker Dinners for Chilly Weeknights

The slow cooker is the friend who shows up with dinner while you're still at work. You do ten minutes of prep before leaving the house, and come home to something that smells like you have your life together. (You do. The slow cooker does.) Every recipe works in a standard 6-quart cooker.

13. Classic Pot Roast with Carrots and Potatoes

SERVES 6 · 15 MIN PREP · 8 to 9 HRS LOW · $$ · SLOW COOKER

A pot roast starts the day as a tough, stubborn chunk of beef and ends it as a tender, emotional experience. Time does the work. It's the only recipe where doing less actually makes it better.

What you'll need

  • 3 lb beef chuck roast
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt, 1 tsp pepper
  • 2 tbsp oil (optional, for searing)
  • 1 lb baby potatoes, halved
  • 4 carrots, cut in 2-inch chunks
  • 1 yellow onion, cut in wedges
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 1/2 cups beef broth
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tsp dried thyme

How to make it

  1. Pat the roast dry and rub with the salt and pepper.
  2. Optional but worth it: heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and sear the roast 3 minutes per side, until deeply browned. If you're short on time, skip it. It will still taste great.
  3. Put the onion, carrots, potatoes and garlic in the bottom of the slow cooker. Set the roast on top.
  4. Whisk the broth, tomato paste, Worcestershire and thyme in a measuring cup and pour it around (not over) the roast.
  5. Cover and cook on LOW 8 to 9 hours (or HIGH 5 to 6 hours), until the meat shreds easily with a fork. Don't lift the lid. Each peek adds 15 to 20 minutes.
  6. Move the roast to a cutting board and let it rest 10 minutes. Shred or slice it, and serve with the vegetables and spoonfuls of the juices from the pot.

Sweater tip: If the meat is tough, it isn't done. Give it another hour. Tough pot roast is just undercooked pot roast.

14. Salsa Verde Pulled Chicken Tacos

SERVES 4 to 6 · 10 MIN PREP · 6 HRS LOW · $ · SLOW COOKER

Three ingredients and a jar. That's the entire plot. This is the recipe for the nights you stare at the fridge and wonder if cereal counts as dinner. (It does not. Make tacos.)

What you'll need

  • 2 lb boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs
  • 1 jar (16 oz) salsa verde
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 8 to 12 small tortillas
  • Toppings: shredded cabbage, cilantro, lime wedges, sour cream, sliced avocado

How to make it

  1. Put the chicken in the slow cooker and sprinkle with the cumin, garlic powder and salt. Pour the salsa verde on top.
  2. Cover and cook on LOW for 6 hours or HIGH for 3 to 4 hours, until the chicken reaches 165°F and shreds easily.
  3. Shred the chicken right in the pot using two forks, pulling in opposite directions. Stir it into the juices.
  4. Let it sit 10 minutes with the heat off. This is when the chicken soaks up all that flavor.
  5. Warm the tortillas in a dry skillet over medium heat, 20 to 30 seconds per side, until soft and a little spotty.
  6. Pile the chicken into tortillas and add toppings.

Sweater tip: Leftovers turn into excellent quesadillas, rice bowls or the world's easiest chicken tortilla soup (add broth and a can of beans).

15. Slow Cooker Beef Stroganoff

SERVES 6 · 15 MIN PREP · 7 to 8 HRS LOW · $$ · SLOW COOKER

Stroganoff is a love letter to the 1970s: creamy, mushroomy and unapologetic. It asks one thing of you, and it's a small thing. Don't dump cold sour cream into a boiling pot, or it curdles and everyone's feelings get hurt.

What you'll need

  • 2 lb beef chuck, cut in 1-inch cubes
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1 lb mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 1/2 cups beef broth
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 cup sour cream, at room temperature
  • 12 oz egg noodles
  • Salt, pepper, chopped parsley

How to make it

  1. In the slow cooker, toss the beef with the flour, 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper until coated.
  2. Add the mushrooms, onion and garlic on top.
  3. Whisk the broth, Worcestershire and Dijon together and pour over.
  4. Cover and cook on LOW 7 to 8 hours, until the beef is fork-tender.
  5. About 15 minutes before serving, cook the egg noodles in salted water according to the package. Drain.
  6. Turn the slow cooker off. Scoop a ladle of the hot liquid into the sour cream and stir. This warms it gently (called tempering) so it won't curdle. Then stir the mixture back into the pot.
  7. Taste and add salt if needed. Serve over noodles and sprinkle with parsley.

Sweater tip: Take the sour cream out of the fridge when you start cooking dinner. Room temperature stays smooth.

16. Apple Cider Pulled Pork

SERVES 8 · 15 MIN PREP · 8 to 10 HRS LOW · $$ · SLOW COOKER

Pork shoulder is a budget cut that behaves like a luxury one if you give it time. Apple cider keeps it juicy and adds a gentle sweetness. It's the only thing in your kitchen that gets more relaxed the longer it cooks, and we can all learn from that.

What you'll need

  • 3 to 4 lb boneless pork shoulder (Boston butt)
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 yellow onion, sliced
  • 1 cup apple cider (the cloudy juice, not hard cider)
  • 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • Buns and coleslaw, for serving

How to make it

  1. Mix the brown sugar, paprika, salt, garlic powder and pepper in a small bowl. Rub it all over the pork.
  2. Spread the onions across the bottom of the slow cooker. Set the pork on top.
  3. Whisk the cider, vinegar and mustard together and pour around the pork.
  4. Cover and cook on LOW 8 to 10 hours (or HIGH 5 to 6 hours), until the pork pulls apart with almost no effort. It should read about 195°F to 205°F inside.
  5. Move the pork to a big bowl and shred it with two forks, discarding large pieces of fat.
  6. Skim the fat off the top of the cooking liquid with a spoon, then stir about 1/2 cup of the liquid back into the pork so it stays juicy.
  7. Pile onto buns with coleslaw, or serve over mashed potatoes.

Sweater tip: Stir in a few spoonfuls of barbecue sauce at the end if you want a smoky, saucy version.

17. Sweet Potato and Red Lentil Curry

SERVES 4 to 6 · 10 MIN PREP · 6 HRS LOW · $ · SLOW COOKER (MEATLESS)

This is the budget-friendly, meatless dinner that even the meat-and-potatoes crowd asks for seconds on. Red lentils melt into the coconut milk and turn the whole pot silky. No one will ask where the meat is. They'll be busy scraping the bowl.

What you'll need

  • 2 medium sweet potatoes (about 1 1/2 lb), peeled, cut in 3/4-inch cubes
  • 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh grated ginger (or 1 tsp ground)
  • 2 tbsp red or yellow curry paste (or 1 tbsp curry powder)
  • 1 can (14 oz) coconut milk
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 2 1/2 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 cups baby spinach
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Cooked rice, for serving

How to make it

  1. Rinse the lentils in a strainer under cold water until it runs mostly clear.
  2. Add everything to the slow cooker except the spinach and lime: sweet potatoes, lentils, onion, garlic, ginger, curry paste, coconut milk, tomatoes, broth and salt. Stir well.
  3. Cover and cook on LOW 6 hours (or HIGH 3 hours), until the sweet potatoes are tender and the lentils have melted into a thick, creamy stew.
  4. Stir in the spinach and cook 5 more minutes, until wilted.
  5. Stir in the lime juice, taste, and add salt if needed. Serve over rice.

Sweater tip: Don't skip the lime. It's what wakes up the whole pot. If it tastes flat, it's usually salt or lime that's missing.

Sheet-Pan Fall Dinners

Sheet-pan dinners are the minimalists of the kitchen: one pan, one oven, one quiet victory. Line the pan with parchment paper or foil and cleanup becomes a rumor. Two beginner rules: don't crowd the pan (food needs space to brown, not steam), and give slower-cooking foods a head start.

18. Sausage, Brussels Sprouts and Sweet Potatoes

SERVES 4 · 40 MIN · $ · OVEN

This is the dinner that turns Brussels sprout skeptics into believers. Roasted hot, the sprouts get crispy and nutty, and the sweet potatoes turn jammy. Add smoky sausage and a maple drizzle, and suddenly everyone's asking for the vegetables.

What you'll need

  • 1 lb pre-cooked smoked sausage or chicken sausage, sliced 1/2-inch thick
  • 1 lb Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
  • 1 1/2 lb sweet potatoes, cut in 3/4-inch cubes
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 1 1/2 tbsp maple syrup

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment or foil.
  2. Toss the sweet potatoes with 1 tbsp oil, 1/2 tsp salt and the paprika. Spread them on the pan in a single layer. Roast 15 minutes. They need a head start.
  3. Meanwhile, toss the Brussels sprouts and sausage with the remaining oil, salt and pepper.
  4. Take the pan out, push the sweet potatoes to one side, and add the sprouts and sausage. Put the sprouts cut-side down for better browning.
  5. Roast 15 more minutes. Stir at the halfway point, then drizzle with the maple syrup for the last 3 to 5 minutes. Added earlier, it burns.
  6. It's done when the sprouts have dark, crispy edges and the sweet potatoes are soft inside. Serve right off the pan.

Sweater tip: If your pan looks crowded, split everything between two pans. Crowded food steams and turns soggy instead of crispy.

19. Maple-Mustard Chicken Thighs with Carrots and Apples

SERVES 4 · 55 MIN · $$ · OVEN

Sweet, tangy, crispy-skinned and weirdly elegant for how little work it takes. The glaze is just maple syrup and mustard, which sounds like a weird couple, but they're basically soulmates.

What you'll need

  • 6 to 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 1/2 lb)
  • 1 lb carrots, cut in 1-inch chunks
  • 1 red onion, cut in wedges
  • 2 firm apples, cut in wedges
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Glaze: 3 tbsp maple syrup, 3 tbsp Dijon or whole-grain mustard, 3 minced garlic cloves, 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar, 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the carrots and onion with the oil and 1/2 tsp salt on a large rimmed baking sheet. Roast 10 minutes.
  2. Pat the chicken thighs very dry with paper towels (that's the secret to crispy skin) and season with the remaining salt and pepper.
  3. Whisk the glaze ingredients in a bowl. Pour half into a separate small bowl and set aside. You'll use this half later. Never reuse glaze that touched raw chicken.
  4. Pull the pan out and nestle the chicken, skin-side up, among the vegetables. Brush the chicken with the glaze from the first bowl.
  5. Roast 20 minutes. Add the apple wedges to the pan, brush the chicken with the reserved glaze, and roast 15 to 20 minutes more.
  6. The chicken is done when the skin is deep golden and a thermometer in the thickest part (not touching bone) reads at least 165°F. Thighs taste best around 175°F.
  7. Rest 5 minutes and spoon the pan juices over everything.

Sweater tip: No thermometer? Pierce the thickest part. The juices should run clear, not pink. Still unsure? Buy the $12 thermometer.

20. Crispy Gnocchi with Sausage and Roasted Squash

SERVES 4 · 40 MIN · $$ · OVEN

Here's the weird magic trick: shelf-stable gnocchi go on the pan straight from the package, no boiling, and come out crispy outside and pillowy inside. It's the dinner equivalent of a plot twist where everything works out.

What you'll need

  • 1 package (16 to 17.5 oz) shelf-stable potato gnocchi
  • 12 oz smoked chicken sausage, sliced
  • 3 cups butternut squash cubes (1/2-inch), pre-cut is fine
  • 1 red onion, cut in wedges
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 1 tsp dried sage or Italian seasoning
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 1/3 cup grated Parmesan
  • 2 cups baby spinach (optional)

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the squash with 1 tbsp oil and 1/2 tsp salt on a large rimmed baking sheet. Roast 15 minutes.
  2. In a big bowl, toss the gnocchi (straight from the package), sausage and onion with the remaining 2 tbsp oil, sage, remaining salt and pepper.
  3. Pull out the pan, push the squash to one side, and spread the gnocchi mixture across the rest of the pan in an even layer.
  4. Roast 18 to 20 minutes, stirring once halfway, until the gnocchi are golden and a little crispy at the edges and the squash is tender.
  5. Sprinkle with the Parmesan. If using spinach, scatter it over the hot pan and stir for 1 minute until it wilts from the heat.
  6. Serve right away, while the gnocchi are at their crispiest.

Sweater tip: Use the shelf-stable gnocchi from the pasta aisle, not the refrigerated or frozen kind. They crisp up differently.

21. Pork Chops with Roasted Cabbage and Apples

SERVES 4 · 45 MIN · $$ · OVEN

Cabbage gets a bad reputation, mostly from people who boiled it into submission in 1987. Roasted, it turns sweet and crispy at the edges, like the best part of a potato chip, but healthy. Pair it with juicy pork chops and sweet apples, and you've got a dinner that looks like it came from a farm-to-table menu.

What you'll need

  • 4 bone-in or boneless pork chops, 3/4 to 1 inch thick
  • 1/2 head green cabbage, cut in 4 wedges, core left in
  • 2 apples, cut in wedges
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • Drizzle: 2 tbsp whole-grain mustard, 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar, 1 tbsp honey

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Brush the cabbage wedges on both cut sides with 2 tbsp oil and sprinkle with 1/2 tsp salt. Place cut-side down on a large rimmed baking sheet. Roast 15 minutes.
  2. While the cabbage roasts, pat the pork chops dry. Rub with the remaining 1 tbsp oil, 1/2 tsp salt, the pepper and the paprika.
  3. Pull out the pan. Flip the cabbage, add the apples, and put the pork chops on the pan.
  4. Roast 15 to 18 minutes, until the pork reads 145°F in the center. Thinner chops will be done sooner, so check early.
  5. Let the chops rest 3 minutes. Resting is part of the cooking and helps keep them juicy.
  6. Stir the mustard, vinegar and honey together and drizzle it over the pork and cabbage.

Sweater tip: The core holds the cabbage wedges together while roasting. Leave it in, and just eat around it.

22. Harvest Chickpeas and Cauliflower with Tahini

SERVES 4 · 40 MIN · $ · OVEN (MEATLESS)

Chickpeas are tiny, round actors who really want to be crunchy. The only thing standing between them and their dreams is water. Dry them well, roast them hot, and you'll get crispy, spiced bites that make croutons look lazy.

What you'll need

  • 1 large head cauliflower, cut in small florets (about 6 cups)
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Tahini sauce: 1/3 cup tahini, 3 tbsp lemon juice, 1 grated garlic clove, 1/4 cup cold water (plus more), pinch of salt
  • Cooked rice or warm pita, and chopped parsley, for serving

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F.
  2. Spread the drained chickpeas on a clean kitchen towel or paper towels and pat them very dry. Wet chickpeas steam instead of crisp.
  3. On a large rimmed baking sheet, toss the chickpeas and cauliflower with the oil, cumin, paprika and salt. Spread in a single layer, using two pans if needed.
  4. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, stirring at the 15-minute mark, until the cauliflower is deeply golden and the chickpeas are crunchy.
  5. Make the sauce: whisk the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt. It will look thick and clumpy at first. That's normal. Keep whisking while adding water, 1 tablespoon at a time, until it's smooth and pourable.
  6. Serve the roasted vegetables over rice or with pita, drizzled with the tahini sauce and sprinkled with parsley.

Sweater tip: Chickpeas lose their crunch as they sit, so serve this one right away.

Fall Flavor Shortcuts: 3 Cheap Tricks

These cost almost nothing and make every dinner above taste like you worked harder than you did.

  1. Brown your butter. Melt butter in a small light-colored pan over medium heat. It foams, the foam settles, and golden-brown specks appear with a nutty smell (3 to 5 minutes). Pour it immediately into a bowl so it stops cooking. Spoon it over soup, roasted squash, pasta or chicken. Cost: about 30 cents. Impact: a restaurant.
  2. Save your Parmesan rinds. Don't throw away the hard cheese ends. Keep them in a bag in the freezer. Drop one into any soup or sauce while it simmers (try recipes 2, 3 or 6), then fish it out before serving. It melts into pure savory depth.
  3. Roast a double batch of squash on Sunday. Cube two squashes, roast at 425°F for 25 to 30 minutes, then cool. Half goes into the week's soup or pasta, and the other half goes in the freezer in a flat bag. Future you will send a thank-you note.
